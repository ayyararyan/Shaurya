## 1. Pre-registration (committed before any result was seen)

### 1.1 Hypothesis under test

**H-DAT20 (Aryan, 2026-08-19 ~13:05 IST).** The depth200 cadence skips measured in `DAT-17`
are not a delivery deficiency. Book activity is concentrated at and near the best bid/offer
and genuinely thins with distance from it. Where depth200 publishes nothing in a ~200 ms
window, nothing changed — it is not the case that a packet was lost. Therefore depth200 is a
**superset** of depth20 and of the Full packet's 5-level block, not a degraded version of
them, and the correct standing statement about the channel is "activity thins with depth",
not "the feed is unreliable".

**Null / incumbent reading (`DAT-17`'s wording).** depth200 is "a quantized, skip-prone
approximately 200 ms base clock" — i.e. the ~401 ms p95 gap is a *skipped tick*, and a
skipped tick may have carried information that was not delivered.

These are not the same claim and they have different consequences for `DAT-09` and `DAT-18`.
If H-DAT20 holds, depth200's blind window is the book's own quiet time and costs nothing. If
it fails, depth200 has a real 400 ms information hole on the instrument we would most want
resolution on.

### 1.2 Confirming prediction (pre-registered)

H-DAT20 is **supported** if all of the following hold:

- **C1 — Top-of-book agreement.** At aligned instants, depth200's own levels 1–5 agree with
  the Full packet's 5-level block, and depth200's levels 1–20 agree with depth20's 20 levels,
  on price, displayed quantity and displayed order count, at a high exact-agreement rate
  under the phase-tolerant alignment rule of §1.5. "High" is pre-registered as ≥ 95% of
  comparable instants for price at level 1, with the full rate reported for every level and
  every field regardless of where it lands.
- **C2 — Monotone decay in level index.** The fraction of depth200 publications carrying a
  change at level *j* decays with *j*, and the mean/median inter-change time at level *j*
  rises with *j*. Decay need not be strictly monotone tick-by-tick; the pre-registered
  requirement is a monotone trend with no reversal larger than sampling noise, tested by
  Spearman rank correlation between level index and change rate (expected strongly negative).
- **C3 — Skips are empty.** For depth200 gaps materially longer than the ~200 ms base tick,
  neither depth20 nor Full recorded a book state inside the gap that depth200 never
  published. Pre-registered as: the rate of "witness state unseen by depth200" inside skip
  windows is not higher than the same rate inside ordinary (non-skipped) windows. This is the
  decisive test; §1.4 states the falsifier for it.

### 1.3 Falsifying prediction (pre-registered)

H-DAT20 **fails** if any of the following hold:

- **F1 — depth200's own top disagrees.** depth200's levels 1–5 disagree with Full's 5-level
  block, or levels 1–20 disagree with depth20's ladder, at aligned instants, at a rate that
  survives the phase-tolerance rule. A residual disagreement that is not explained by clock
  phase is a genuine content difference, and a superset cannot differ from its subset.
- **F2 — depth200 misses top-of-book changes the cheaper tiers recorded.** A top-of-book
  change is visible in depth20 or Full and is absent from depth200's published sequence
  entirely (not merely delivered one tick later). That is loss, not thinning.
- **F3 — Skips are not empty.** Inside skipped depth200 windows, depth20 or Full witness book
  states that depth200 never published, at a rate materially above the non-skip baseline.
- **F4 — No decay.** Change rate is flat in level index, or does not fall below the cheaper
  tiers' cadences within 200 levels. That would mean deep levels are as active as the BBO,
  contradicting the mechanism H-DAT20 proposes.

### 1.4 What each outcome would mean (stated in advance)

| Outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| C1+C2+C3 all hold | depth200 is a superset; the standing description changes to "activity thins with depth". `DAT-17`'s "skip-prone" wording is retained as a *delivery-cadence* description but is explicitly stripped of any implication of information loss. |
| C1, C2 hold; C3 fails | Activity does thin, and the top of book is consistent, but the skips still hide states. depth200 is then a superset *in the levels it publishes* and lossy *in time*. H-DAT20's central claim fails. |
| F1 or F2 | H-DAT20 fails on the superset claim itself. depth200 would be a different, not merely coarser, view. |
| C3 holds but C1 fails | Unlikely combination; would indicate an alignment-rule error rather than a feed property, and would be reported as such rather than as evidence for either side. |

### 1.5 Alignment rule (stated explicitly, not left implicit)

Reusing `DAT-15`'s discipline (`src/shaurya/data/alignment_analysis.py`): comparisons are
**backward-looking by default** — no look-ahead — and phase tolerance is applied as an
explicit, separately reported second pass, never silently mixed into the primary number.

Every tape row on the depth20 and depth200 channels already carries the *merged* book (both
sides, latest known levels for each), with `update_side` naming the side just refreshed. A
row is therefore a complete book state as of its own `receive_ts`. This is a property of
`DhanLiveStream._emit_deep` and is verified in the tape rather than assumed.

Three alignment passes will be reported separately:

1. **Pass A — strict backward.** For each witness instant *t* (a depth20 burst timestamp, or
   a Full row carrying a 5-level block), the depth200 comparand is the last depth200 row with
   `receive_ts <= t`. Agreement is exact equality of price, quantity and order count at each
   compared level. Staleness of the comparand is recorded and reported; comparisons where the
   comparand is older than 1,000 ms are excluded and counted separately.
2. **Pass B — phase-tolerant bracket.** The same witness instant agrees if **any** depth200
   row with `receive_ts` in *[t − T, t + T]* matches, for T ∈ {250, 500} ms. A disagreement
   under Pass A that resolves under Pass B is classified **phase offset between two clocks**.
   A disagreement that survives Pass B at T = 500 ms is classified **genuine content
   difference** and counts toward F1.
3. **Pass C — reverse direction.** For each depth200 publication instant, the same comparison
   with depth20/Full as the comparand, so the result is not an artifact of which tier is
   treated as the reference clock.

All three tiers are captured in **one process writing one tape**, so all `receive_ts` values
come from a single local clock and no cross-machine clock skew enters the comparison. The
residual is per-channel socket and parse latency, which is not identified by this design and
is stated as a limitation rather than corrected.

### 1.6 Skip-window test (measurement 3) — procedure fixed in advance

- A depth200 **burst** is a set of rows sharing one exact `receive_ts` (`DAT-16`/`DAT-17`
  method).
- Successive burst gaps are computed. A gap is a **skip** if it exceeds a pre-registered
  threshold of 300 ms — i.e. at least one ~200 ms base slot produced nothing. Gaps ≤ 300 ms
  are the **non-skip control set**.
- For every window, both skip and control, the witnesses are (i) every Full row with a
  5-level block whose `receive_ts` falls strictly inside the window, and (ii) every depth20
  burst falling strictly inside the window.
- For each witness state inside a window, the top-5 (Full) or top-20 (depth20) ladder is
  compared against the depth200 state that *opened* the window and the depth200 state that
  *closed* it. Three outcomes:
  - **matches the opening state** — nothing had changed at the witness instant; consistent
    with H-DAT20.
  - **matches the closing state** — the change had already occurred and depth200 published it
    at the next tick; a coalescing/latency effect, not a lost state.
  - **matches neither** — a third, intermediate state existed that depth200 never published.
    This is the F3 event.
- The F3 rate inside skip windows is compared against the F3 rate inside control windows.
  **If the two rates are statistically indistinguishable, the skip is not special and C3
  holds. If the skip-window rate is materially higher, F3 fires and H-DAT20 fails on its
  central claim.** The control comparison is pre-registered precisely so that a nonzero raw
  F3 count cannot be read as failure without the baseline.
- Note recorded in advance: depth20's own 500 ms clock cannot resolve inside a single 200 ms
  slot, so **Full is the only tier with the timing granularity to act as an inside-window
  witness**. depth20 is reported but is expected to contribute few inside-window
  observations. This is a known power limitation of the test, stated before the result.

2. **Change rate by level index** — for *j* = 1…200, bid and ask separately, the fraction of

### 1.8 Scope boundary fixed in advance — what H-DAT20 cannot reach

H-DAT20 addresses the **cadence skips on a single subscribed instrument**. It does not and
cannot explain `DAT-17`'s separate finding that on a four-future socket, positions 2–4 emitted
one startup bid/ask pair and then stayed silent for ~89.9 s. Those were four front-month index
futures with continuously active BBOs; "activity thins away from the BBO" says nothing about
why an entire instrument, including its top of book, went silent. `DAT-13` established that
dominance follows subscription **position** and rotates when the order rotates, which is a
subscription throttle, not a liquidity effect.

**Therefore, whatever this task finds, the observed usable recurring depth200 ceiling of 1
instrument per socket stands unchanged.** No result below may be read as softening, merging or
relitigating that finding. If evidence bearing on it turns up, it is reported as a separate
finding with its own evidence, not as a corollary of H-DAT20.

## 2. Verdict first, in plain English

**Aryan's reinterpretation is right about the thing that matters, and it is right for a
different reason than the hypothesis states.**

The decisive question was whether depth200's ~400 ms gaps hide book states. They do not. When
depth200 skips a tick, the top of book inside that gap is exactly as well described by the
publications on either side of it as it is when depth200 *does* publish inside a gap of the same
length. Ten independent tests across two runs, all non-significant. So the 401 ms p95 gap is not
an information hole: nothing was delivered because nothing needed delivering. **The standing
description of depth200 should stop implying loss.** "Skip-prone" is accurate about the delivery
clock and should be kept as such, but it must be stated explicitly that a skipped tick is a quiet
book, not a dropped packet.

Where the hypothesis is wrong is in its stated mechanism. Activity is **not** concentrated at the
BBO on this instrument. Measured per price point, activity peaks a few rupees *behind* the best
quote — in the Rs 2–10 band — and only then decays. The best quote itself sits in a thin,
single-lot region: the median spread on the NIFTY August future in this window was **Rs 5.00**
(run 1) and **Rs 6.70** (run 2), so there is usually no price point at all within Rs 1 of mid.
Activity does thin with distance, which is the part that matters for the width-versus-depth
decision, but it thins away from a *shoulder* a few rupees out, not away from the touch.

Two things Aryan asked about turned out differently from the working assumption. First, depth200
always publishes all 200 levels — there is no padding and no partial ladder. Second, those 200
levels span far more price than assumed: median **Rs 136.0 and Rs 136.8** from deepest bid to
deepest ask, roughly **3.4× the ±Rs 20 (Rs 40) working assumption** and **13.7× the Rs 10-per-side
contiguous-tick arithmetic**. The levels are heavily gapped — only 13–18% of the 0.05-rupee tick
grid inside that span is occupied, about one price point every 6–8 ticks.

The direct answer to `DAT-09`: **86.6–99.0% of all book events happen within Rs 20 of the
same-side best quote, and Rs 20 is a median of 74–90 occupied levels.** So roughly the first 90
levels per side carry almost everything, and levels beyond that — over half the ladder — carry
1.0–13.4% of events at 20× to 1,000× lower intensity per price point. That is the number the
width-versus-depth trade should be priced against.

**One thing this task does not change: the 1 instrument per socket ceiling stands, untouched.**
See §9.

### 3.4 Corrections and additions made during analysis

Four issues were found while analysing run 1. Each is a correction to a *measurement*, not to the
hypothesis or to a pre-registered prediction, and each is disclosed here with its effect. All four
carry regression tests. The pre-registration at `bc458d4` was not edited.

**(1) Price quantisation — a real bug in the first pass, corrected.**
*Previous claim, from the first analysis pass:* depth200 and the Full 5-level block agreed on
price at 0 of 791 aligned instants.
*Corrected claim:* they agree at 75.5% (run 1) and 86.0% (run 2) strict, 96.5% and 99.0%
phase-tolerant, at level 1.
*Why the previous number was wrong:* the Full packet encodes its 5-level prices as IEEE-754
**binary32** (`FIVE_LEVEL_STRUCT` = `"<IIHHff"`) while both depth channels encode **binary64**
(`DEEP_LEVEL` = `"<dII"`). A price of 24118.9 therefore arrives as 24118.900390625 on Full and as
24118.9 on depth200. Exact float equality between the tiers is structurally impossible, so the
0/791 figure measured the encoding, not the book. Prices are now quantised to 2 decimal places,
which is exact for the NSE 0.05-rupee tick: the binary32 representation error near 24,000 is about
0.002 rupees against a 0.005 rounding half-width, so no genuine one-tick difference can be masked.
*What else was audited after finding it:* every cross-tier comparison in the task, in both
directions; the depth20-versus-depth200 comparisons are unaffected because both use binary64 from
the same wire encoding. The Full quantity field is `H` (uint16, max 65,535); the largest quantity
observed on any tier in either run is well inside that, so no truncation occurred. Two regression
tests now pin the behaviour, including one asserting that a genuine one-tick difference still
registers after quantisation.

**(2) Set containment added.** Positional equality is the wrong test for a superset claim: a
single price point present in one feed and absent from the other shifts every level beneath it, so
one extra level makes all deeper positions mismatch. Containment asks the question H-DAT20
actually poses — is every level the cheaper tier published also present in depth200's ladder? This
is reported in §4.2 alongside, never instead of, the pre-registered positional numbers.

**(3) Price-keyed activity added.** The pre-registered measurement 2 counts a change at level *j*
when the triple at *position* j differs. That definition cannot separate genuine activity at a
price point from the positional cascade an insertion higher up forces onto every deeper level.
Since C2 and F4 are stated in terms of activity thinning, the position-keyed curve alone cannot
adjudicate them. A price-keyed measurement is reported in §5.2. Both results are given, and they
point in **opposite directions** — see §5.3.

**(4) Duration-matched skip test added.** The pre-registered skip-versus-control contrast in §1.6
is confounded with window length: a skip window is about 400 ms and a control window about 200 ms,
so a witness inside a skip window has twice as long for the book to move away from both endpoints
even if nothing was lost. The confound is real and large — it accounts for the entire apparent
effect. §6.2 holds duration fixed instead. Both the pre-registered test and the duration-matched
test are reported.

Two readings follow, and they must be kept apart.

- **In coverage, depth200 is unambiguously a superset.** It spans a median Rs 136.0–136.8 of price

  explains why this design cannot attribute it.

### 4.3 Characterising the residual — and one thing this test cannot decide

Three sources of positional disagreement were separated, and one could not be.

1. **Clock phase — identified and large.** The ±500 ms bracket resolves 490 of 687 all-level price
   disagreements against depth20 in run 1, and 166 of 192 at level 1. This is the dominant
   component and it is not a feed defect.
2. **Positional cascade — identified.** One inserted or removed price point shifts every deeper
   level. Diagnosed directly: containment is high (median 0 missing) where positional agreement is
   only 47–51% strict, which is only possible if the ladders hold the same price points in shifted
   positions.
3. **Bid/ask asymmetry — identified as churn, not as a content defect.** Ask-side agreement is
   consistently worse than bid-side, in every direction and both runs (best-bid 97.0–97.2% versus
   best-ask 80.2–86.9% at ≤30 ms in run 1). The explanation is in §5.2: near the touch the ask side
   ran at 1.4–2.8 events per second per price point against 0.6–1.0 on the bid, so the ask side
   decorrelates faster under any residual phase error. Critically, this is **not** depth200 missing
   inside levels — the implied spread distribution is identical across all three tiers to within
   0.04 rupees (§7.1), which it could not be if depth200 were dropping levels near the touch.
4. **Snapshot phase versus content — NOT DISCRIMINATED.** Every row on every channel in both runs
   carries `exchange_timestamp_missing`, and `source_sequence_unavailable` on all 11,770 and 11,802
   rows. There is no exchange timestamp and no source sequence anywhere in this tape. Receive-time
   alignment can therefore bound *delivery* skew but cannot align *snapshot instants*: depth20
   publishes on a 500 ms clock, so its content can be up to ~500 ms stale relative to its own
   receive time, invisibly. The 1–6% of bid price points and 6–18% of ask price points that one
   tier reports and the other does not are consistent with pure snapshot-phase misalignment and

### 5.3 Why the two curves disagree, and which one answers the question

They measure different objects, and only one of them is the object C2 and F4 are about.

The position-keyed curve counts a change at level *j* whenever the triple at *position* j differs.
When a single new price point appears near the top of the book, every level beneath it shifts by
one position, so all ~199 deeper positions register a "change" from one event. At a 4.16/s
publication rate this saturates: the deep-level positional rate plateaus at 3.1–3.4/s, which is
simply ~76–82% of the publication rate — the fraction of publications in which the ladder shifted
anywhere. That plateau is a property of the publication clock, not of the deep book.

The price-keyed curve asks whether a *given price point* becomes quieter as it moves away from the
best quote. That is exactly the mechanism H-DAT20 proposes, and the answer is yes.

The regression test `test_activity_by_distance_is_price_keyed_and_survives_a_positional_cascade`
pins this: on a fixture where one level is inserted at the top and no deep price point moves, the
positional measurement reports a change at every deeper level while the price-keyed measurement
reports exactly one event.

**Both results are reported. The pre-registered metric fails C2 and fires F4; the corrected metric
meets C2 and does not fire F4. The correction was made because the pre-registered metric is
provably confounded, not because it gave an unwelcome answer** — and it was diagnosed from the
data, with a test, before the verdict was written.

### 5.4 The DAT-09 input, stated in level indices

Aryan asked for "the depth beyond which a whole dedicated socket buys nothing a cheaper tier
wasn't already delivering". Two answers, because the two metrics give different ones.

- **On the pre-registered position-keyed metric: there is no such level.** The rate never falls
  below depth20's 2.00/s or Full's 1.2–1.7/s at any index to 200. Reported as measured; §5.3
  explains why it is the wrong metric for this decision.
- **On the price-keyed metric: the boundary is about Rs 20 from the touch, which is a median of
  74–90 occupied levels per side** (§7.2). Inside it lies 86.6–99.0% of all book activity. Beyond
  it, a price point runs at 0.05–0.08 events/s on the bid and 0.002–0.003 on the ask, against
  1.2–2.8 near the touch.

Stated for the decision: **levels beyond roughly index 90 per side — over half of the 200-level
ladder — carry 1.0–13.4% of book events at 20× to 1,000× lower intensity per price point.** Note
that this does not translate into "depth20 would have sufficed": depth20's 20 levels reach only
Rs 7.4–10.1 from mid (§7.2), which captures 57.0–84.1% of bid activity and 67.2–82.8% of ask
activity, not 86.6–99.0%. The band between depth20's edge and Rs 20 out is real and only depth200
sees it. That is a `DAT-09`/`DAT-18` decision, not a conclusion this task takes.

Depth200 cadence in these runs, reproducing `DAT-17` closely: 2,718 and 2,764 distinct receive

exclude a small one: at these sample sizes the level-1 tests would detect an absolute increase of
roughly 4–6 percentage points with conventional power, so an effect smaller than that is **not
discriminated**. The test also cannot speak to gaps beyond the observed 603 ms maximum.

## 9. The 1 instrument per socket ceiling is unaffected

`DAT-17` found that on a four-future socket, subscription positions 2–4 emitted one startup
bid/ask pair and then stayed silent for about 89.9 s, and that the observed usable recurring
depth200 ceiling is therefore **1 instrument per socket**.

**Nothing in DAT-20 bears on that finding, and it stands unchanged.** DAT-20 tested a single
subscribed instrument on a dedicated socket. H-DAT20 is a claim about *where in the book* activity
sits; the DAT-17 finding is about *which subscription position* receives a feed at all. Those four
instruments were front-month index futures with continuously active BBOs, so "activity thins away
from the best quote" cannot explain an entire instrument going silent including its top of book —
and `DAT-13` already established that dominance follows subscription **position** and rotates when
the subscription order rotates, which is a throttle, not a liquidity effect.

No evidence bearing on the ceiling was found in this task. Had any been found it would be reported
here as a separate finding with its own evidence, not as a corollary of H-DAT20. The ceiling is
not softened, merged, or relitigated.

## 10. Scope limits

Stated so no result is read wider than it was measured.

- **One instrument.** NIFTY August 2026 future only. Nothing here covers other underlyings.
- **Futures only.** No options. Option books are thinner and gappier and none of §5 or §7
  transfers to them.
- **One time-of-day window.** 13:09–13:32 IST, midday. `DAT-16`'s and `DAT-15`'s caveats about
  time-of-day generalisation apply unchanged. No open, no close, no post-news period.
- **One liquidity state.** Median spread Rs 5.00–6.70, frequently single-lot top levels. The
  location of the activity peak in §5.2 is conditional on this.
- **No reconnect period.** Zero reconnects in both runs by design. Behaviour across a reconnect,
  including whether the ladder resynchronises cleanly, is untested here.
- **No exchange timestamp anywhere.** The single largest limitation. All 23,572 rows across both
  runs carry `exchange_timestamp_missing` and `source_sequence_unavailable`. Snapshot-instant
  alignment is therefore unidentified and F1/F2 cannot be adjudicated (§4.3(4)).
- **Two runs, 22 minutes total.** Both replicate, which is real evidence of stability, but 22
  minutes of one instrument on one day is not a general characterisation of the channel.
- **`ANL-03` held one Quote/Full socket throughout.** A different endpoint from depth200 and from
  depth20, so contamination is considered unlikely — stated rather than assumed away, exactly as
  `DAT-17` recorded it.
- **Residual ladder-window slide.** The 5-level boundary margin in §5.2 did not fully remove the
  window-slide artifact in the deepest band; the [50,∞) bid tail is non-monotone as a result. It
  is reported rather than trimmed, and it does not affect the Rs 20 conclusion.

## 13. Interpretation correction from Aryan — what the quiet far tail is for

Aryan clarified at approximately 13:59 IST that the far depth200 ladder is not being collected
primarily to estimate routine fill probabilities or cancellation behaviour. Its intended research
use is **rare-event monitoring**: because the region is normally quiet, an unusual addition,
removal, relocation, displayed-quantity shock or order-count shock may carry information about
subsequent price movement.

That interpretation changes the value assigned to the low unconditional event rate, but it does
not change any DAT-20 measurement:

- DAT-20 shows the channel is reliable enough to observe the deep ladder and finds no evidence
  that the ordinary ~400 ms gap represents information loss.
- The sparse far-tail baseline is potentially useful for anomaly detection rather than wasted
  coverage.
- DAT-20 **does not** establish that a rare deep event is large, directional or predictive, nor
  does it identify the relevant future-price horizon. It contains only two 11-minute midday runs
  on one future and was designed to test feed loss, not predictability.

The prediction question is therefore a new task, `SIG-21`, under D28: pre-register causal,
price-keyed deep-book anomalies and test their future mid-price response with past-only baselines,
dependence and multiple-testing controls, D27-admissible horizons, and a multi-session power gate.
