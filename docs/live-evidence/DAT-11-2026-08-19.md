## Addendum — solo per-instrument delivery rate

The accepted ceiling candidates showed nearly invariant per-instrument delivery: the NIFTY and
BANKNIFTY controls each received 116 packets in almost every 15-second window, including NIFTY's
116 packets inside the 50-instrument subscription. To distinguish a per-instrument delivery cap
from shared socket bandwidth or an unconstrained event rate, NIFTY-Aug2026-FUT (`58072`) was then
subscribed **alone** twice. Each run used a fresh socket and exactly one one-instrument message.

| Fresh solo run | Window (IST) | Packets / 15 s | Packets/s | Ratio to 50-instrument reference |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 11:06:44–11:07:00 | 116 | 7.73 | 1.000 |
| 2 | 11:07:00–11:07:16 | 120 | 8.00 | 1.034 |

The predeclared rule was: both solo counts within ±10% of 116 implies
`per-instrument-rate-cap`; both at least 25% above implies `genuine-event-rate`; otherwise
`not-discriminated`. Both solo runs remained within 3.4% of the 50-instrument reference.

**Addendum conclusion: per-instrument rate cap.** Removing 49 instruments did not increase
NIFTY's delivered rate materially, so the observed ~8 packets/s cadence is not explained by
shared socket bandwidth. At the measured ceiling this implies an approximately 125–129 ms
delivered view for a liquid future, rather than an event-by-event book stream. Any exchange events
collapsed inside that interval are unidentified from this feed. The direct implication for D23 is
that queue-ahead and cancellation/trade decomposition must retain a partially identified interval
at least as wide as this delivered cadence; it cannot treat each received book as every underlying
event.

**Residual risk:** the solo runs occurred about 55 minutes after the 50-instrument reference, so
they do not hold latent market activity fixed. The exact repetition at 116 and the independent 120
run are strong cap evidence under the requested design, but not a broker guarantee across dates or
feed releases. Raw credential-free artifact:
`data/live-evidence/2026-08-19/dat11-solo-rate-addendum.json` (local ignored evidence store).

## Correction to the addendum conclusion (DAT-16, same day)

**Previous claim (above):** the observed ~8 packets/s implies "an approximately 125-129 ms
delivered view for a liquid future".

**Corrected claim:** the delivered view updates at **2.00 book states per second, a 501 ms
cadence**. The blind window is ~500 ms, not ~125 ms.

**Why the previous claim was wrong.** It treated each received packet as one book state. It is
not. Grouping the two healthy DAT-14 captures by distinct receive timestamp gives 1,195 bursts in
597.9 s on *both* NIFTY and BANKNIFTY — 2.00/s, burst-gap p05/p50/p95 of 496/501/506 ms, a
metronome rather than a distribution — with a mean of 4.17 tape rows inside each burst. The 116
packets per 15 s that this addendum treated as the discriminating quantity is exactly that clock:
15 s x 2 bursts/s x ~4.17 rows/burst = ~125 rows. It is a snapshot cadence, not a bandwidth cap
and not an event rate.

**What survives.** The addendum's measurement and its predeclared decision rule stand: two solo
runs at 116 and 120 packets/15 s against a 116 reference confirm that per-instrument delivery does
not improve when 49 instruments are removed, so shared socket bandwidth is ruled out. Only the
conversion from packet rate to delivered-view interval was wrong.

**Effect.** Every consequence the addendum drew for `D23` holds but is four times worse: the
partially identified interval for queue-ahead and cancellation/trade decomposition must be at
least **500 ms** wide, not 125-129 ms. Full detail, reconciliation against DAT-15's quote-age
distribution, and the consequences for `EXE-09`/`EXE-10` are in
`docs/live-evidence/DAT-16-2026-08-19.md`.
