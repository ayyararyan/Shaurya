## Plain English

### The direct answer to Aryan's question

> **How much of the next futures mid move does simple level-one CKS pressure explain, after
> controlling for how much depth is at the touch?**

**Very little on its own, and rather more once you divide by depth — but neither survives the
checks.**

| Question | Answer on these two recordings |
|---|---|
| Raw CKS level-one OFI, added to a causal depth control | Best cell adds **+1.44 percentage points** of held-out R² (1-second window → next 2 seconds). Positive in 11 of 25 cells. |
| Does dividing by depth help? | **Yes, clearly.** Depth-scaled pressure beats raw OFI in **22 of 25 cells**, and its best cell adds **+6.01 points** (2-second window → next 2 seconds). |
| Does anything survive the three overlap-aware checks? | **Barely, and not meaningfully.** 2 of 25 raw-OFI cells and 1 of 25 pressure cells clear all three, and each of those either flips sign between the two recordings or is worth under half a point. |
| Does it reproduce across both recordings? | **No.** The one raw-OFI cell that clears the checks is **+1.72 ticks per standard deviation in the first recording and −0.32 in the second.** |
| Is it the same thing `X-OFI-DAT20-03` found? | **No.** That scan's 10-second top-10 lead is reproduced here (**+7.16 points** over the same depth control). Level-one contributes **−0.54 points** at that cell, with a *negative* coefficient. |

**Bottom line in one sentence:** on 22 minutes of one falling session, the simple CKS best-quote
variable explains on the order of one percentage point of the next one-to-two seconds of mid
movement above a depth control, depth scaling multiplies that to roughly six points at its best
cell, and neither result reproduces across the two recordings — so this is a **weak exploratory
observation, not a signal**.

### The thing that most changes how to read all of it

**The touch in these recordings is not a touch in the usual sense.** The median best-bid to
best-ask spread is **100 ticks (₹5.00)** in the first recording and **134 ticks (₹6.70)** in the
second, on a full 200-level ladder on both sides. Level one is therefore a lone quote sitting at
the front of a wide gap, not a queue that traders jostle for.

The transition decomposition shows exactly that:

| What actually moves the level-one CKS number | Share of total absolute contribution |
|---|---:|
| Best-**quote price moves** (the best price itself relocating) | **80.9%** |
| Same-price displayed **size** changes (additions and removals) | **19.1%** |

and it is almost entirely one-sided:

| Side | Share |
|---|---:|
| Ask side | **81.4%** (84.6% in recording 1, 77.2% in recording 2) |
| Bid side | 18.6% |

The best bid is unchanged in **91.3%** of valid transitions; the best ask in **80.1%**. In a
falling session with a ₹5–7 spread, the level-one CKS variable is mostly *"where did the best ask
move to"*. Any explanatory power it shows should be read as that, not as queue dynamics.

### What is and is not identifiable here

Dhan sends **snapshots, not order messages**. That fixes hard limits, stated before the results
were computed:

- **Gross limit-order arrivals: not identified.** Between snapshots an add and a cancel at the
  same price cancel out; only the net is visible.
- **Gross cancellations: not identified.** A fall in displayed size at an unchanged best price can
  be a cancellation, an execution, or both.
- Every same-price increase is therefore reported as a **displayed addition** and every same-price
  decrease as a **displayed removal**. Neither word appears as "arrival rate" or "cancellation
  rate" anywhere in the code or artifacts.
- **The trade split does not resolve it.** The tapes carry 310 trade packets with 52,715 contracts
  of identified executed volume, 307 of them side-classified. But level-one same-price displayed
  removals total only **29,120 contracts**, while level-one price-move depletion totals **112,905**.
  Executed volume therefore *exceeds* the same-price removals it could be matched against — the
  vendor's trade packets are coalesced, far slower than the depth feed, and carry no level
  attribution. The honest result is that **no clean execution-versus-cancellation split is
  identified on this feed**, and the artifact records the share as a saturated upper bound rather
  than pretending otherwise.

### The sign convention, with a worked example

Positive means buy pressure. Concretely, if between two snapshots the best bid holds its price and
grows from 200 to 500 contracts while the best ask holds its price and shrinks from 500 to 200,
then `e = +300 + 300 = +600` contracts: bid strengthening and ask depletion are both positive.
Reverse both and `e = −600`. If the best bid jumps up to a new price holding 300, that is `+300`;
if it falls away from a price that held 300, that is `−300`. If the best ask falls to a new price
holding 300, that is `−300`; if it retreats upward from a price that held 300, that is `+300`.

## The four things Aryan asked to be told plainly

### 1. Does depth scaling help? Yes.

`M4b` (depth + pressure) beats `M3` (depth + raw OFI) in **22 of 25 cells**. At the strongest cell,
2-second window into the next 2 seconds, dividing by average level-one depth takes the increment
from **+1.27 to +6.01 percentage points**. Pressure alone reaches **6.31%** held-out R² there. The
coefficient is **+0.84 ticks per unit of pressure**, or **+1.70 ticks per training standard
deviation**, in the theory-aligned positive direction.

That is the cleanest positive finding in this scan, and it is exactly the CKS prescription:
imbalance means more when the resting depth it moves against is smaller.

**But it does not clear the bar.** At that cell the three dependence checks read **1.74 / 1.54 /
2.09** — two of three below 1.96. The only pressure cell that clears all three is 10-second window
into the next 2 seconds, worth **+0.34 points**, which is nothing.

There is also a warning inside the number. At that same cell, in-sample R² for `M4b` is **1.36%**
while out-of-sample is **5.30%**. Out-of-sample exceeding in-sample by a factor of four is not
strength; it means the held-out tail happened to be a favourable stretch of one session.

### 2. Does any apparent power survive past and same-window controls?

**The past-mirror placebo behaves better than it did for `X-OFI-DAT20-03` — and that is the one
genuinely encouraging detail here.** Across the grid the past increment beats the future increment
in **13 of 25 cells**, but the split is not random:

- At the **short windows and short horizons where level-one OFI actually works** (0.5–2 s window,
  1–5 s horizon), the past increment is mostly **negative**: −1.13, −1.95, −2.94, −0.39, −1.20,
  −1.66 points. The placebo does **not** fire where the result is.
- At the **long windows** (5 s and 10 s) the past dominates enormously: **+12.94, +10.92, +9.51,
  +7.43, +6.55** points. Those cells are measuring the session's fall, not prediction, and should
  be discarded on sight.

The same-window construction diagnostic behaves as expected and confirms the arithmetic without
being evidence of anything:

| `h1` | Same-window R², raw OFI | Same-window R², pressure |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 s | 0.91% | 1.16% |
| 1 s | 0.45% | 3.02% |
| 2 s | −0.12% | 3.18% |
| 5 s | 6.92% | 14.99% |
| 10 s | 11.85% | 27.70% |

Same-window OFI and price change are partly mechanical, so these are never called forecasting
power. Note the oddity that same-window raw OFI at 2 seconds is slightly **negative** while the
2-second-into-2-second future cell is the strongest raw cell — another reason not to over-read
either number on this sample.

### 3. Does it reproduce across both recordings? No.

This is where the scan fails hardest.

The one raw-OFI cell that clears all three dependence checks — 2-second window into the next
2 seconds, +1.27 points, `t` = 2.27 / 2.36 / 2.53 — has:

| Recording | Increment over depth | Coefficient (ticks per training SD) | Coefficient (ticks per 100 contracts) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `sha-...6ca41203` | +0.036 points | **+1.72** | +0.319 |
| `sha-...286d5105` | −0.005 points | **−0.32** | −0.051 |

**The sign flips.** The pooled result is one recording, not two. The second surviving cell
(0.5 s → 10 s) is worth +0.038 points and is likewise inconsistent. Across the whole grid, no cell
combines a materially positive increment, three surviving statistics, *and* the same sign in both
recordings.

The strongest raw cell, 1-second into 2 seconds (+1.44 points), does at least agree in sign
(+1.67 and +0.54 ticks per SD) and is positive in both recordings — but its statistics are
1.95 / 1.85 / 2.14, so it does not clear the bar either.

### 4. How does this compare with `X-OFI-DAT20-03`?

The comparison is carried inside this scan as model `C1`, the earlier scan's price-keyed top-10
OFI fitted against the *same* causal depth control on the *same* 5,204 observations. It is not a
re-search of that 175-cell grid.

| At 10-second window → next 10 seconds | Increment over the depth control |
|---|---:|
| Price-keyed top-10 OFI (`X-OFI-DAT20-03`'s lead) | **+7.16 points** |
| CKS level-one OFI | **−0.54 points** |

The earlier lead is confirmed to be robust to swapping its spread-and-microprice baseline for a
depth baseline: it was +7.91 points before and is +7.16 points here, on 8.40% held-out R². It is
therefore genuinely a **levels-2-to-10 phenomenon** and genuinely **not** a level-one phenomenon —
which is what the earlier nested ladder already said, now confirmed against the canonical CKS
level-one object rather than inferred from a depth cutoff.

More than that: at that cell the level-one coefficient is **negative** (−3.03 ticks per training
standard deviation pooled; −2.08 and −6.29 in the two recordings). Over 10 seconds, best-quote
pressure in these recordings ran *against* the following move while flow behind the touch ran
*with* it. On one session that is an observation, not a mechanism.

Across the grid the two objects split by horizon: price-keyed top-10 beats level one in **13 of 25**
cells, concentrated at 5–30 second horizons; level one beats it in the other **12**, concentrated at
1–2 second horizons. That is the sensible reading — the touch carries very short-lived information,
the near book carries the longer-lived kind — but on 22 minutes it is a hypothesis, not a result.

### The separately labelled robustness arm

`R1` adds spread and microprice tilt to `M3`, reproducing `X-OFI-DAT20-03`'s baseline so the two
scans' controls can be compared. It is reported separately and deliberately does **not** redefine
the primary depth-controlled comparison, which remains `M3` versus `M2`.

`R1` beats `M3` in **15 of 25 cells**. Its best cells are all at the 30-second horizon — 2.08%,
1.96% and 1.79% held-out R² at 0.5, 1 and 2-second windows — where `M3` is at or below zero. Spread
and microprice tilt are therefore carrying long-horizon information that neither the depth control
nor level-one flow captures. That is a note for a future design, not a result: with the past-mirror
placebo firing hard at long horizons on this session, a 30-second cell here cannot be believed.

## Limitations, stated plainly

1. **22 minutes, one instrument, one falling session, outcomes already inspected.** Nothing here is
   confirmed, tradeable, causal, economic, or representative. `confirmatory_eligible` is `false`.
2. **A 100–134 tick median spread.** Level one in these recordings is not a contested queue. A
   normal-spread session could produce an entirely different level-one object.
3. **25 cells searched.** Two cells clearing an unadjusted 1.96 bar out of 25 is close to what
   chance produces, and no multiplicity adjustment would rescue them.
4. **Out-of-sample above in-sample** at the strongest pressure cell (5.30% vs 1.36%) indicates a
   favourable test tail, not a strong relationship.
5. **Snapshot identification.** Gross arrivals and gross cancellations are unidentified, and the
   trade fields do not deliver a clean execution split — see above.
6. **602 test observations pooled** (265 and 337 by recording). The non-overlapping block counts
   behind the statistics are as low as 13 at the 10-second horizon. Several checks are underpowered
   and are reported as such rather than smoothed over.

## Bottom line

Simple CKS level-one net pressure explains **about one percentage point** of the next one-to-two
seconds of futures mid movement above a causal depth control on these recordings, and **about six
points at its best cell once divided by depth** — depth scaling clearly helps, in 22 of 25 cells.

But the best raw cell **flips sign between the two recordings**, the best pressure cell **fails two
of three dependence checks and scores higher out of sample than in sample**, and the object itself
is dominated by best-ask price relocation inside a ₹5–7 spread rather than by queue dynamics.

`X-OFI-DAT20-03`'s lead survives this scan's independent depth control at **+7.16 points**, and
level one contributes **nothing** to it. The correct conclusion is that **the earlier scan was right
to locate the lead behind the touch**, and that the canonical CKS level-one variable is not an
alternative source of it on this session.

**Status: exploratory observation. Not a signal, not a candidate to register.**

### Object construction

For each consecutive valid depth-200 snapshot pair inside one connection epoch,
`e = 1{P^B_n ≥ P^B_{n-1}} q^B_n − 1{P^B_n ≤ P^B_{n-1}} q^B_{n-1} − 1{P^A_n ≤ P^A_{n-1}} q^A_n
+ 1{P^A_n ≥ P^A_{n-1}} q^A_{n-1}`, in contracts, decomposed into the eight components tabulated
above with an exact reconstruction assertion. Transitions are discarded for non-depth-200 channel,
non-monotone receive time, connection-epoch change, invalidating quality flags, an empty side, or a
crossed or locked book — the same rules `X-OFI-DAT20-03` uses, reused unchanged. A window is
emitted only when its complete past lies inside one epoch with no discarded transition in it.

The depth control and the scaling denominator are both measured at or before the window end; an
assertion in `assert_no_lookahead` and a test enforce that no window starts after its own
observation or reaches back further than its own length.
